01Problem

A new brand, no reviews, and no card payments yet.

Yem's is a young house with its own atelier in Cotonou and a curated line of shoes chosen elsewhere. The site had to sell to a man who cares how he looks at work, in a market where most people pay on delivery or by Mobile Money, and before the business had a merchant account.

And it had to earn people's trust without faking anything: no made-up reviews, no photos of shoes that hadn't been made yet.

02Approach

Order now, pay later. Price on the server.

  • No payment at checkout. The order is saved, the atelier is notified on WhatsApp and calls the customer back. Payment happens on delivery or by Mobile Money. Bespoke pieces need a 50% deposit, because the leather is bought first.
  • The cart says what, never how much. Every price is recalculated on the server from the catalogue, so a tampered cart can't change an amount.
  • Being upfront. Pieces that can't be photographed yet sit behind an announcement page instead of empty cards. Early fake reviews were replaced by « Personne ne nous a encore recommandés » and three ways to check the atelier yourself.
  • Online payment ready, but off. Card and Mobile Money payment through KkiaPay is implemented and verified server-side, and stays switched off until a merchant account exists.

03Build

Built by hand, from one catalogue file.

The catalogue lives in a single JSON file. A Python generator writes 23 static pages from it: product pages, categories, sitemap and structured data. A Cloudflare Worker handles orders, the admin API and live catalogue edits, which are applied to the pages at the edge without a rebuild.

  • Bespoke configurator: shape, leather, thread, sole, size and initials, with an itemised price that updates live
  • Admin for the atelier: order statuses, WhatsApp links, catalogue overrides and data anonymisation
  • Responsive images (WebP, srcset), non-blocking fonts and immutable caching
  • Accessibility: skip link, visible focus, reduced motion, and a contrast checker that runs through the CSS cascade
  • A security pass: content security policy, CSRF and brute-force protection, data minimisation

05Real outcome

Where it stands.

The site is built and documented, and the shop hasn't launched yet. It has 23 generated pages, a working configurator, an ordering flow, an admin, legal pages, and a step-by-step deployment guide written for the owner.

What I can't show you. I don't have visit or sales figures to share: the site runs no analytics, on purpose, and online payment isn't switched on yet. The product photos on the shop are AI-generated, and the site says so.
